MOBILE PRODUCT · UX/UI · PROTOTYPE

BrewBlitz

Bringing pickup ordering, home subscriptions, and rewards together for people fitting good coffee into busy routines.

Overview

BrewBlitz explores how home coffee subscriptions and on-the-go ordering could work as one cohesive mobile experience. I led the concept from competitive research and persona development through information architecture, wireframes, brand identity, and a high-fidelity interactive prototype.

Role

Product designer (UX Research, Brand Identity & UI Systems)

Tools

Adobe XD, Photoshop & Illustrator

The Problem

Ordering coffee for pickup and replenishing beans at home are separate routines. The concept asks how one product could make both easier to manage without burying frequent actions in a complex menu.


Design goals

Make repeat pickup orders quick to find; connect in-store ordering, subscriptions, and rewards; and carry one clear visual system across the experience. These are design goals for the prototype, not measured product outcomes.

Identifying the Market Gap

Research insights

The competitive review explored a gap between coffee subscription services and cafe ordering experiences. It informed a concept that brings pickup, subscriptions, and rewards into a shared product rather than treating them as unrelated destinations.

These insights informed my decision to prioritize a unified Smart Dashboard, ensuring home brewing and on the go habits were managed in one place.

Identity

Logo / Icon / Color Palette / Font Style

To support the product concept, I developed a visual language that balances artisanal coffee cues with the clarity and speed expected of a mobile utility.

Brand Scale

Functional Packaging / Direct to Consumer / Internal culture

BrewBlitz isn't just an app, it's an experience. I designed BrewBlitz's visual identity to scale across physical mediums, ensuring that whether the user is brewing at home or grabbing a cup in the city, the brand feels cohesive, premium, and efficient.

Functional Packaging (Tote and Carrier):

The tote bags and coffee carriers utilize the minimalist espresso and cream palette to maintain a high end aesthetic in public spaces, turning every pickup into brand building.


Direct to Consumer (Box and Beans):

The subscription packaging features integrated QR codes that link back to the app's Reorder section, bridging the gap between physical consumption and digital management.


Internal Culture (Apparel):

The staff uniform utilizes the high contrast Action Teal on their sleeves, ensuring employees are easily identifiable to users rushing in for a Quick Order, reducing time on task friction.

Next Steps

This project pushed me to think beyond a single interface and design for a brand that lives across app, packaging, and physical space. The central design challenge was balancing an artisanal feel with the speed and simplicity expected in the Quick Order and Home Brew flows.

Next, I would test the ordering and subscription flows with users, then refine the hierarchy and interaction details based on observed friction.
