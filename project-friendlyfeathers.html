Friendly Feathers

Exploring a bird-first app that brings care discovery, adoption, species information, and community into one experience.

Overview

I led Friendly Feathers from market research and persona development through information architecture, wireframing, and high-fidelity UI. The app concept brings practical tools—such as vet discovery and adoption—together with a species guide and community features.

The Problem

Bird owners may need to piece together care information, veterinary options, and community support across different sources. The concept explores how a bird-specific product could make those resources easier to discover.

Design direction

Bring care discovery, adoption, species information, and community into one bird-first experience. The prototype illustrates this direction; it does not claim clinical verification or measured user outcomes.

Role

Solo UX/UI designer

Tools

Adobe Photoshop, Figma

Identifying the Market Gap

Understanding the landscape

I explored how bird owners navigate care, adoption, and community, then mapped the opportunity against existing platforms. Research and persona artifacts helped frame the product direction; the prototype represents an exploration, not a clinically verified care resource.

Identity

Voice / Color Palette / Font Style / Components

I developed the FriendlyFeathers identity to balance warmth with credibility. Soft, approachable visuals and breathable layouts create a sense of safety and compassion, while structured typography and clean UI patterns reinforce trust and authority. Social features are intentionally designed to feel curated and purposeful, supporting meaningful connection without overwhelming the experience. The result is a focused ecosystem where community and expert guidance coexist seamlessly.

Next Steps

With 96 screens spanning vet finding, adoption, community and a species database, the hardest part of FriendlyFeathers wasn't any screen; It was making four different tools feel like one product instead of four apps stitched together. I leaned on the persona and competitive research to keep decisions anchored: every feature had to answer a real need Rachel identified.

Moving forward, I'd want to test the information architecture directly with users; specifically, whether they can move between booking a vet appointment and browsing the Birddex without the app feeling disjointed.
